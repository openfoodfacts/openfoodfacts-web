The Genesis: Making Nutrition Labels Speak

Open Food Facts launched on May 19, 2012. Within weeks, a fundamental question arose: how can open food data deliver immediate, concrete value to citizens? In late 2011 and early 2012, French parliamentary debates on the 'soda tax' (enforced January 1, 2012) were raging. Yet beyond the ubiquitous red-and-white Coca-Cola can, what was actually inside the thousands of sodas sold across supermarkets?

Operation Sodas served as the very first collaborative proving ground to test the emerging community's energy, team up with data journalists, and build innovative visual exploration tools.

The 5 Core Citizen Inquiries in Supermarket Aisles

❓ Is there a difference between major brands and private labels or discount sodas?
💡 Nutrition figures are very similar, but budget brands sometimes substitute natural extracts with artificial flavorings or sweeteners.
❓ What is the sugar cube and caffeine equivalent in a standard can?
💡 A standard 330ml can of classic cola packs the equivalent of 7 sugar cubes (around 35g)!
❓ Are sodas sweeter in French Overseas Territories than in mainland France?
💡 This pioneering question anticipated national public health debates and the 2013 Lurel law banning excess sugar formulas in overseas territories.
❓ What ingredients are unique to energy drinks like Red Bull?
💡 Heavy loads of taurine, caffeine, glucuronolactone, and B-vitamins coupled with massive sugar doses or synthetic sweeteners.
❓ Are organic sodas nutritionally advantageous, and which ones suit vegans?
💡 Organic sugar remains sugar metabolically. For vegans, vigilance is required regarding cochineal carmine (E120) insect-derived coloring in red sodas.

💡 From Operation Sodas to 3.8 Million Products
Operation Sodas proved that citizen crowdsourcing could map an entire grocery category in just a few days. This methodology laid the blueprint for What's in my yogurt? (2014), What's in my shampoo? (2016), and the vibrant Scan Parties organized across Europe today.
