1. General

Introduction

Open Food Facts is a collaborative, non-profit open data initiative gathering information on food products worldwide. All data is published under an open data licence (ODbL), allowing anyone to freely reuse it to build innovative applications, services, scientific research, and health tools.

Today, there are hundreds of re-uses—from health and diet calculators to connected devices, epidemiological research, and investigative journalism.

The two core sets of information gathered are nutrition facts (energy, fat, sugars, proteins, etc.) and ingredients, complemented by data such as labels (organic, vegetarian, AOC, fair trade, etc.), packaging codes, and origins. Whether you are a newcomer or an experienced contributor, this guide serves as your reference to input high-quality data.

Sister Projects

The French-language edition of Made Near Me, tracking local manufacturing and packing codes.

An educational game challenging players to guess the sugar quantity in common food products (also available in French at Combien de SUCRES?).

2. Contributors Overview

Citizens and organizations contribute to the Open Food Facts database in multiple complementary ways:

  • Mobile Applications (Android & iOS): Quickly scan barcodes in stores or at home, snap photos, and add initial product data.
  • Web Platform (openfoodfacts.org): Ideal for advanced tasks:
    • Adding comprehensive product information not readily editable on mobile apps
    • Adding products without barcodes (fruits, vegetables, bulk items, local bakery goods)
    • Batch photo uploads from high-resolution digital cameras
    • Using mass upload tools to import catalogs or contributor collections
⚡

3. Open Food Facts 101

When contributing via mobile app or web, follow this essential 3-step photography rule:

📸
The 3 Essential Photos:
  1. Front of package: Shows the brand, product title, and net quantity clearly.
  2. Ingredients list: Captures the full text including highlighted allergens.
  3. Nutrition facts table: Captures values per 100g/100ml and per serving.

Once these three photos are uploaded, the essential information is safely stored on Open Food Facts servers. Our automated optical character recognition (OCR) and contributor community can extract data even if you don't type it right away!

If you have a few extra moments, take photos of additional package details:

  • Labels & Certifications: Organic (Bio), Fair Trade, Nutri-Score, vegetarian/vegan seals.
  • Packaging & Traceability Codes: EMB / health marks, QR codes, manufacturer addresses.
  • Official URLs: Manufacturer websites or customer service contact info.
💡
Stay Logged In: Always ensure you are signed into your Open Food Facts account both in the app and on the website. This lets you track your personal contribution history, earns contributor badges, and makes it easy to find and complete your products later.

4. The App & Website

The Mobile Apps

Open Food Facts offers official native apps for iOS and Android. They are open source, ad-free, and privacy-respecting.

Get Android App ↗ Get iOS App ↗

The Website

After scanning initial items on mobile, visit openfoodfacts.org to complete fields, verify automated text recognition, and curate product details.

Basic Principles

  • Strict Package Fidelity: Enter only information explicitly printed on the package. Do not guess ingredients or extrapolate origins.
  • Photos are paramount: High-resolution, unedited photos allow everyone to verify and improve data over time.
  • Every contribution counts: If you only have time to take photos, that is already tremendously valuable!

5. Step-by-Step Example

Walk through the complete lifecycle of uploading and enriching a product on the Open Food Facts website:

1

Log in and capture photos with your phone

Ensure you are logged into your account in the Open Food Facts mobile application. Scan the barcode, then capture clear photos of the front, nutrition table, and ingredients list.

2

Find your uploaded product on the website

Open openfoodfacts.org in your browser. When logged in, click on the link “Products you added that need to be completed” in the navigation header or user menu.

Click on the product card you wish to complete to open its editing form.

3

Crop and normalize the front photo

Select the front photo in the photo management interface. Using your mouse, drag the selection handles to enclose only the product, excluding distracting table surfaces or background clutter:

Click “Validate and/or resize image”. Enabling the “Normalize colors” checkbox enhances contrast and readability for most cameras.

4

Fill in general product information

Enter details exactly as printed on the package. Fill in as many fields as available:

Enter the commercial name in Product name. If relevant, add everyday colloquial terms in Common name (or leave blank if unnecessary).

✨
Normalized Autocomplete Fields: Fields such as Categories and Labels are normalized across languages. Start typing a keyword to select standard entries from the drop-down suggestion list:
5

Crop and transcribe the ingredients

Crop the ingredients photo so the text is sharp and legible. Type all ingredients, respecting two key formatting rules:

⚠️
Formatting Rules for Ingredients:
  • Comma separator: Always separate ingredients with commas (,), even if the package uses dashes, bullet points, or semicolons.
  • Allergen highlighting: When allergens are in bold, uppercase, or italics on packaging, wrap the allergen name with underscores (e.g., _wheat flour_, _skimmed milk_).
💡
Layout Tip for Side-by-Side Transcription: If the crop appears side-by-side or below the text box depending on your monitor resolution, adjust your browser width slightly so the photo sits directly adjacent to or above the text input for easiest side-by-side typing:
Before (Side layout)
After (Stacked layout)
6

Enter nutrition facts

Transcribe the nutrition values (Energy, Fat, Saturated Fat, Carbohydrates, Sugars, Proteins, Salt, Fiber) as printed on the table.

If the product does not specify nutritional values (e.g., vinegar, table salt, culinary herbs, spices, or certain alcoholic beverages), tick the box “Nutrition facts are not specified on the product”.

7

Save and review the product page

Click the Save button at the bottom of the form. A confirmation banner will confirm the successful save.

Click “See the product page” to inspect the computed Nutri-Score, Eco-Score, NOVA group, and check for any transcription errors. Congratulations, your contribution is live! 🎉

6. Each Field in Detail

Language & Photo Management

Main Language

Configuration
Sets the primary language of the packaging text. If your mobile app uploaded data in another language, switch to the correct language using the drop-down selector before editing textual fields.

Manage Images

Media Tools
Allows you to view all uploaded raw photos, delete duplicates, re-orient rotated images, or copy/move photos between barcodes if pictures were mistakenly submitted to the wrong product.

Add Language (Multilingual Products)

Multilingual
Many products display multilingual packaging (e.g., French/Dutch in Belgium or English/Spanish in the US). Click “Add Language” to add dedicated language tabs for the title, generic name, and ingredients without creating duplicate products.

Product Characteristics

Product Name

Essential
The primary commercial title printed prominently on the package (e.g. “Choco Crispy Flakes”).

Common Name / Generic Name

Optional
The standard regulatory or descriptive descriptor of the food (e.g. “Toasted rice flakes with cocoa”). Leave blank if no specific common descriptor appears on the box.

Quantity

Measurement
Net quantity with unit of measurement. Common patterns:
Examples:
  • 400 g or 1 L: Standard net weight/volume.
  • 400 g (drained: 235 g): Canned goods with net and drained weights.
  • 6: Count-based products without weight (e.g., fresh eggs).
  • 180 capsules: Dietary supplements or tea capsules.

Packaging

Eco-Score
Packaging components and materials (e.g., “Cardboard box”, “Plastic tray”, “Aluminium foil”). This directly impacts the Eco-Score packaging score.

Brands

Essential
Brand names as printed on the packaging. Multiple brands can be separated by commas (e.g., store brand and sub-brand).

Categories

Taxonomy
Categories are standardized. Select suggestions from the autocomplete drop-down list. Choosing the most specific category (e.g., “Dark chocolates with hazelnuts” rather than just “Chocolates”) ensures accurate Nutri-Score calculation.
💡
Need categorization guidance? Ask fellow contributors in the #general channel on Open Food Facts Slack.

Labels, Certifications & Awards

Taxonomy
Quality marks, environmental certificates, dietary seals, and origins (e.g., Organic, EU Organic, Fairtrade, Halal, Kosher, Gluten-free, Nutriscore Grade A). Start typing to pick verified labels from the taxonomy.

Origin of Ingredients & Processing Places

Traceability
Enter origin indications explicitly printed on the package (e.g., “Milk from Brittany, France”, “Manufactured in Spain”). Leave blank if no explicit geographical origin is mentioned.

EMB Code / Health Mark / Factory Code

Traceability
In the European Union, the oval sanitary mark containing country code, department/region, and factory approval number (e.g., FR 29.039.020 CE). For eggs, producer codes indicate farming method and country (e.g., 1UK21006, 0FR12345).

Official Product URL

Reference
URL to the product's official page on the manufacturer's website.

Best Before Date / Expiry

Date Format
The recommended standardized entry format is: <DDMMMYYYY> (for example: 02NOV2026).

City, State & Store of Purchase

Local Data
Store brand (e.g. Tesco, Carrefour, Monoprix) and geographical location where you purchased or scanned the item.

Countries Where Sold

Do Not Guess
Indicate only countries where you know the product is sold. Do not guess: multilingual text does not guarantee commercial distribution in every corresponding nation (e.g., Swiss packaging may include German, French, and Italian without being sold in Germany, France, or Italy).

Ingredients Field Conventions

The ingredients list enables automated additive detection, allergen alerts, and ultra-processing (NOVA) scoring:

  • Separate ingredients with commas: Even if packaging uses bullet points, dashes, or semicolons.
  • Wrap allergens with underscores: For example, _wheat flour_, _soy lecithin_, _peanuts_.
  • Transcribe percentages: Retain percentage figures (e.g., “strawberries (15%)”) to assist Nutri-Score & fruit/vegetable proportion calculations.

Nutrition Facts Table

Enter nutritional data per 100g or 100ml. If non-standard nutrients are present on the packaging (such as specific vitamins, calcium, magnesium, omega-3, or polyols), start typing in the nutrient field to select the normalized nutrient name:
