Three Complementary Pillars

Our Environmental Scoring Efforts

Rather than inventing proprietary black-box ratings, Open Food Facts operationalizes peer-reviewed public science and open-source calculation engines across three complementary environmental indicators.

A+ to F Grade

Green-Score

Co-founded in 2021 (originally as Eco-Score, renamed Green-Score at the end of 2024), this front-of-pack grade summarizes 15+ Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) indicators from ADEME & INRAE’s Agribalyse database.

  • Baseline LCA: Agriculture, processing, packaging, transport, distribution, and consumption.
  • Bonus & Malus Modulators: Official eco-labels (Organic, Fair Trade, MSC/ASC), ingredient origins, threatened species, and packaging circularity.
  • European Scale: Partnering in the FOODTURE consortium to expand harmonized LCA coverage across 31 European countries.

Deforestation Risk

Forest Footprint

Built in collaboration with the environmental NGO Envol Vert, the Forest Footprint quantifies the surface area of tropical and temperate forest threatened with deforestation to produce a food item’s ingredients.

  • Embedded Soy in Feed: Estimates soy feed required for chicken, eggs, and livestock products—even when labeled “Packed in Europe”.
  • Expanded Risk Commodities: Tracks palm oil, cocoa, coffee, beef, and risky ingredients with origin-aware risk factors.
  • Certification Offsets: Credits verified deforestation-free, GMO-free, organic, and Label Rouge supply chains.

Ecobalyse Engine

Coût Environnemental

Open Food Facts integrates directly with France’s official open-source Ecobalyse calculation engine (developed under ADEME & the Ministry for Ecological Transition) to compute granular Coût Environnemental impact points.

  • Automated API Pipeline: Real-time Ecobalyse computation triggered on product edits with smart caching.
  • PEF-Aligned Multi-Criteria: Aggregates 16 environmental impact dimensions into a single comparable impact cost.
  • Granular Inputs: Maps taxonomy categories, ingredient ratios, storage conditions, and packaging specs.

Raw Crowdsourced Data

Millions of citizens, producers, and researchers upload barcodes, front/back packaging photos, raw ingredient strings, nutrition tables, and recycling symbols from supermarkets worldwide.

Digital-to-Physical Impact

From Open Database to Supermarket Aisles: Colruyt Belgium

Open data achieves its highest impact when it leaves the screen and meets shoppers at the exact moment of choice.

Pioneering In-Store Environmental Transparency

In 2021, Belgian retail leader Colruyt Group became the first major supermarket chain in Belgium to deploy the Eco-Score (now Green-Score) across its physical stores and digital channels—bridging the gap between open-source food science and everyday grocery shopping.

  • Electronic Shelf Labels & Packaging: Shoppers see the color-coded Green-Score directly on store shelf tags, aisle posters, and Colruyt private-label packaging (Boni Selection, Boni Bio, Spar, Graindor, Everyday) as well as partner brands.
  • Omnichannel Xtra Integration: Consumers can scan barcodes in-store with the Xtra app or filter online shopping lists by environmental score, while cross-checking full breakdowns on Open Food Facts.
  • Incentivizing Reformulation: Putting an A–E grade on physical shelves creates immediate market pressure for suppliers to switch to lower-impact ingredients, local sourcing, and recyclable packaging.

Under the Hood

The Heavy Lifting: Decoding Hidden Supply Chains

Accurate environmental scoring requires solving four notoriously messy data-engineering challenges that most databases ignore.

🏭

Mapping Factories via Traceability Codes

European and international packages carry cryptic oval health approval marks and packer codes (such as FR 56.251.001 CE or EMB 29150), as well as MSC/ASC fishery codes and FAO marine fishing zones. Open Food Facts decodes and links these identifiers to official veterinary and fishery registries to pinpoint the exact processing plant, country, and catch area.

Barcode → Health Mark (FR 56.251.001 CE) → Exact Plant & Region → Transport & Grid Footprint
🧭

Detecting Origins from Ingredients & On-Pack Claims

Origin information is scattered across front-of-pack badges (“Made in Belgium”), legal origin lines (“Milk: EU / Non-EU”), and parentheticals inside ingredient lists (“tomatoes (Italy)”, “cocoa (West Africa)”). Our parsers extract and normalize these multilingual clues into structured country and region tags to compute transport impact and national environmental policy scores.

"Hazelnuts (13%, Turkey), Cocoa (Ivory Coast)" → origins: [tr, ci] → Country & Transport Modulators
🗂️

Fine-Grained Categorisation for Specific LCA

A generic “dairy” or “snacks” bucket is useless for Life Cycle Assessment: beef lasagna has a vastly different footprint from vegetable lasagna, and oat yogurt differs fundamentally from cow’s milk yogurt. Open Food Facts classifies products into thousands of fine-grained taxonomy nodes—plus storage conditions (ambient, chilled, frozen)—so each product matches its exact Agribalyse / Ecobalyse LCA archetype.

"Plant-based fermented oat dessert" → en:oat-yogurts → Specific Agribalyse / Ecobalyse LCA ID
🦧

Detecting Palm Oil Under All Its Variations

Pioneered by Open Food Facts in 2012, our ingredient analysis engine unmasks palm oil even when disguised behind technical, fractional, or chemical names across dozens of languages—while also recognizing certified sustainable palm oil labels (e.g., RSPO) to distinguish uncertified deforestation risk from verified supply chains.

Matches: "palm oil", "palm kernel oil", "palmitic acid", "sodium palmate", "fractionated palm fat", E471*
Reverse-Engineering the Food Chain

Creating the Missing Data Out of Thin Air

Manufacturers rarely print exact ingredient percentages, packaging polymer weights, or full supply-chain logistics on the box. Instead of waiting years for mandatory disclosure, we infer missing data transparently and scientifically.

Why Inference Matters: “Avoid Waiting for Industry or Government to Do the Work”

When exact data is absent, refusing to calculate a score leaves consumers in the dark. By combining legal labeling rules, category baselines, citizen science, and conservative fallbacks, Open Food Facts turns incomplete packaging into actionable environmental estimates—while clearly flagging where exact producer data would improve the score.

📊

Recipe & Percentage Estimation

Ingredients are legally listed in descending order of weight. Using ingredient rank, known declared percentages, nutrition facts constraints, and category recipe profiles, we estimate missing ingredient proportions to run LCA and Forest Footprint calculations.

📦

Packaging & Material Inference

Through citizen science campaigns like Mission Emballages, volunteers weighed and cataloged hundreds of thousands of containers. When a product lacks packaging weight, category-level packaging ratios by format fill the gap cleanly.

🌿

Taxonomic & Conservative Fallbacks

When a sub-ingredient or origin is unspecified, our engine inherits properties from parent ingredients or applies conservative en:unknown origin footprints—rewarding brands that disclose verified origins and penalizing opacity.
