## What is the Green-Score (formerly Eco-Score) methodology?

*Note: Since late 2024, the Eco-Score environmental rating has been renamed the **Green-Score**.*

The Green-Score is based on life cycle analysis (LCA) data produced by ADEME (the French ecological transition agency) and INRAE (national research institute for agriculture, food and environment). About fifteen environmental indicators are combined to calculate the Product Environmental Footprint (PEF) benchmark score.

To this PEF score, bonuses and penalties are applied (certified organic or agroecological farming, raw ingredient geographic origins, impact on endangered biodiversity, and packaging recyclability) to calculate the final Green-Score grade from A to E.

For more information, see the [Green-Score overview page](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/green-score) on Open Food Facts.

---

## What type of information must be provided for the Green-Score to be calculated as accurately as possible?

For the Green-Score to be calculated with the highest precision, the percentage of each ingredient and its country of origin should be specified.

If this information is not already present in the ingredient statement, producers can provide it with the following structure:

- Wheat flour 16% (France)
- Chocolate 8% (Ivory Coast, Ecuador)

Detailed packaging composition is also essential: e.g. 1 plastic film to discard, 1 FSC cardboard box to recycle, 6 transparent PET bottles 1.5 L, 6 opaque caps, 12 aluminum cans 33 cl.

---

## How are the values of the 15 environmental indicators combined?

To learn more about the Life Cycle Assessment (LCA / PEF) system, you can consult the [Agribalyse documentation](https://doc.agribalyse.fr/documentation/methodologie-acv) and the [Open Food Facts Green-Score methodology](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/green-score).

---

## Does the local score aspect concern the place of manufacturing or the provenance of raw materials?

The local transportation aspect primarily evaluates the **provenance of agricultural raw materials**. This is why specifying ingredient countries of origin on product records is essential.

---

## How to enter the origins of ingredients for the Green-Score?

To share ingredient origin details, include them directly in the ingredient text (e.g. "hazelnuts (Italy)") or fill out the dedicated origin fields on the producers platform or mobile app.
