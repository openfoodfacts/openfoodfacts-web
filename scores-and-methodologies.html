Principles of Openness & Science

Why openness, independent governance, and peer-reviewed rigor are non-negotiable.

Explore Open Data ➔

For more than a decade, Open Food Facts has demonstrated that food data and public health algorithms belong in the public domain. Unlike proprietary scoring systems or private for-profit databases, our entire methodology rests on five unbreakable pillars:

🔓 100% Open Data (ODbL)

All product data, ingredient taxonomies, nutritional breakdowns, and environmental estimations are published under the Open Database License (ODbL). Citizens, universities, NGOs, and commercial startups can freely inspect, download, and build upon our datasets.

💻 Transparent Open Source Code

There are zero black-box algorithms. Every calculation rule, nutritional cut-off point, and edge case is published in our open-source repositories on GitHub. Anyone can audit how a grade is computed and verify its mathematical correctness.

🔬 Grounded in Academic Science

We do not invent proprietary scores out of thin air. We partner with established academic research teams (including EREN, Inserm, INRAE, ADEME) and implement scientifically validated, peer-reviewed standards recognized by international health organizations.

🛡️ Independent & Non-Profit

Open Food Facts is a registered non-profit organization. We accept no advertising, sell no consumer data, and offer no paid score upgrades to manufacturers. Our ratings are completely independent of food industry lobbying.

🤝 Citizen Science Ground-Truthing

Our database is crowdsourced and cross-checked by tens of thousands of active volunteers. By photographing actual product packaging and barcodes, our community creates an auditable historical paper trail that guarantees factual ground-truth.

📚 Over 500+ Scientific Papers

Epidemiologists, climate scientists, and economists worldwide rely on Open Food Facts to study ultra-processing trends, food reformulation, and national dietary patterns. Browse our dedicated Scientific Publications index.

Nutri-Score: Nutritional Quality from A to E

Front-of-pack nutritional grading reflecting overall dietary healthfulness.

Nutri-Score Guide ➔

The Nutri-Score is a voluntary front-of-pack nutrition logo that translates the mandatory nutritional declaration table (calories, sugars, fats, sodium) into an intuitive 5-color, 5-letter grade from A (dark green) for foods with high nutritional quality to E (dark orange) for foods to consume in moderation.

Developed by EREN (Equipe de Recherche en Epidémiologie Nutritionnelle, Paris 13 University / Inserm / INRAE) under the leadership of Professor Serge Hercberg, and adopted officially by public health authorities across 7 European nations (France, Belgium, Germany, Spain, Netherlands, Luxembourg, Switzerland).

The Balance Formula:
Negative Points (0 to 40): Unfavorable nutrients that should be limited (high energy density, saturated fatty acids, sugars, and sodium).
Positive Points (0 to 15): Favorable components to encourage (percentage of fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts, specific oils like olive and rapeseed, dietary fiber, and protein).
Final Score: Score = Negative Points − Positive Points (lower is healthier).

The New 2024–2025 Nutri-Score Algorithm

To keep pace with the latest European dietary guidelines and discourage deceptive industrial reformulation, an international scientific steering committee updated the algorithm:

  • Better drink differentiation: Milk, flavored dairy drinks, and plant-based beverages are now classified under the beverage category with stricter sugar thresholds.
  • Sweetener penalty: Artificially sweetened diet sodas are downgraded (from B to C/D/E) to discourage replacing sugar with chemical sweeteners.
  • Stricter salt & sugar cut-offs: Highly sweetened breakfast cereals and salty snacks face sharper downgrades.
  • Fatty fish & healthy oils rewarded: Olive oil, rapeseed oil, walnut oil, and oily fish (salmon, sardines) see improved ratings.
  • Red meat limitations: Red meat scores are capped to reflect European anti-cancer nutritional recommendations.

NOVA: Degree of Food Processing & UPF

Distinguishing authentic culinary ingredients from industrial ultra-formulations.

NOVA Classification Guide ➔

Created by Professor Carlos Monteiro and his epidemiological team at the University of São Paulo (Brazil), the NOVA classification looks beyond nutrient numbers to assess how food is altered by industrial manufacturing.

Group 1: Unprocessed or Minimally Processed

Whole edible parts of plants, animals, fungi, and water. Processing involves only drying, freezing, milling, or pasteurization without added salt, sugar, or fats (e.g. fresh fruits, oats, plain yogurt, frozen vegetables).

Group 2: Processed Culinary Ingredients

Substances extracted directly from nature or Group 1 foods through pressing, refining, or mining. Used in kitchens to season and cook real food (e.g. butter, cold-pressed olive oil, cane sugar, table salt).

Group 3: Processed Foods

Relatively simple foods manufactured by adding salt, sugar, oil, or vinegar to Group 1 foods to enhance preservation or taste (e.g. canned sardines in olive oil, artisan bread, simple cheeses, canned legumes in brine).

Group 4: Ultra-Processed Foods (UPF)

Industrial formulations made mostly from deconstructed substances (maltodextrins, hydrogenated fats, protein isolates) combined with cosmetic additives (flavor enhancers, emulsifiers, artificial colors, texturizers).

Public Health Importance: Massive prospective cohort studies (including the French NutriNet-Santé cohort published in The BMJ) have linked regular UPF consumption to significantly higher risks of cardiovascular disease, obesity, type 2 diabetes, depression, and overall mortality. National dietary guidelines in Brazil, France, Canada, and Belgium now explicitly recommend limiting or avoiding Group 4 ultra-processed foods.

Green-Score: Environmental Impact from Farm to Fork

The transparent ecological rating evaluating life cycle impacts, packaging, and biodiversity.

Full Technical Methodology ➔

The Green-Score (formerly Eco-Score) is an environmental grade from A (very low impact) to E (very high impact) designed to help consumers minimize the planetary footprint of their shopping basket. Food production accounts for over 28% of global greenhouse gases, 70% of freshwater consumption, and significant biodiversity degradation.

Calculated by an open collective including Open Food Facts, Yuka, Marmiton, ScanUp, ECO2 Initiative, and La Fourche, it couples official government Life Cycle Assessments with product-specific environmental modulators.

The Two-Tier Calculation Framework

  1. LCA Category Benchmark (Agribalyse v3): Using data compiled by ADEME and INRAE covering 16 European PEF environmental indicators (carbon emissions, water scarcity, land use, ozone depletion, acidification, eutrophication). Normalized to a 0–100 scale using a logarithmic formula.
  2. Complementary Product Modulators (±25 pts):
    • Production Systems (+5 to +20 pts): Rewarding Organic (AB, Demeter), Label Rouge, Fairtrade, and HVE certifications.
    • Local Sourcing & Freight (0 to +15 pts): Geodesic distance tracking, road/rail/maritime modal mix, and ADEME Base Carbone factors.
    • Environmental Policy (−5 to +5 pts): Origin score based on the Yale/Columbia Environmental Performance Index (EPI).
    • Packaging Circularity (0 to −15 pts): Upstream recycled content incorporation and downstream municipal recyclability.
    • Threatened Species Safeguards: Hard caps for depleted fish stocks and −10 penalty for unsustainable palm oil.
Mandatory Packaging Gate: No food product can receive a Green-Score A unless its packaging is proven to be 100% recyclable or biodegradable.

Forest Footprint: Embedded Deforestation Risks

Tracking the hidden forest destruction behind livestock feed, soy, and palm oil.

Forest Footprint Details ➔

In collaboration with the environmental NGO Envol Vert, Open Food Facts computes a dedicated Forest Footprint for food products. This metric quantifies the surface area of tropical and temperate forest threatened with deforestation or degradation to produce the ingredients in our food.

A vast proportion of deforestation in South America (the Amazon and Cerrado biomes) is driven by intensive monoculture soy cultivation, which is exported globally to feed chickens, laying hens, dairy cows, and pigs.

How the Forest Footprint is Calculated

  • Animal Feed Conversion: Estimates the mass of chicken meat or eggs contained in the food product, calculating the soy feed required throughout the animal's life cycle.
  • Geographic Sourcing Risk: Cross-references ingredient origin with deforestation-risk regions (e.g. Brazilian Cerrado vs. European-grown domestic soy).
  • Certification Offsets: Takes into account agricultural labels (Organic / Bio, Label Rouge, Bleu-Blanc-Cœur, GMO-free animal feeding) that certify deforestation-free or locally sourced feed.
  • Direct Forest Commodities: Evaluates palm oil, cocoa, coffee, and beef with commodity-specific land conversion factors.

Even if an egg or chicken breast packaging mentions "Packed in Europe", the livestock was frequently fed with imported South American genetically modified soy associated with primary forest clearing. The Forest Footprint brings visibility to this invisible ecological cost.

UK Traffic Lights: Front-of-Pack Nutrient Warning Lights

At-a-glance color coding for fat, saturated fat, total sugars, and sodium.

Traffic Lights Guide ➔

Established by the United Kingdom's Food Standards Agency (FSA), the Traffic Lights nutritional system assigns simple Green (Low), Amber (Medium), or Red (High) color codes to four critical macronutrients: Fat, Saturated Fatty Acids, Sugars, and Salt.

Unlike Nutri-Score (which synthesizes positive and negative nutrients into a single global letter), Traffic Lights allow shoppers to spot specific dietary red flags instantly—ideal for individuals managing diabetes (sugar), hypertension (salt), or cholesterol (saturated fat).

Official UK FSA Thresholds (per 100g of Food)

Nutrient 🟢 Low (Green) 🟡 Medium (Amber) 🔴 High (Red)
Total Fat ≤ 3.0 g > 3.0 g to ≤ 17.5 g > 17.5 g (or > 21g / portion)
Saturated Fat ≤ 1.5 g > 1.5 g to ≤ 5.0 g > 5.0 g (or > 6g / portion)
Total Sugars ≤ 5.0 g > 5.0 g to ≤ 22.5 g > 22.5 g (or > 27g / portion)
Salt (Sodium × 2.5) ≤ 0.30 g > 0.30 g to ≤ 1.50 g > 1.50 g (or > 1.8g / portion)

🎯 Dual Per-100g and Per-Portion Rules

The FSA standard applies strict per-portion thresholds in addition to per-100g rules. If a single serving of a product exceeds 21g of total fat, 6g of saturated fat, 27g of sugars, or 1.8g of salt, it automatically triggers a Red light, preventing manufacturers from manipulating portion sizes to hide excessive nutrient densities.

Synergy with Nutri-Score: A food might achieve an overall Nutri-Score C, but feature a Red light on salt. Having both indicators side-by-side provides consumers with a complete nutritional radar.

1. From a Research Paper to Reality (2014)

In 2014, when Professor Serge Hercberg’s team proposed the "5-Color Nutrition Grade" in a public health report, food industry lobbyists claimed it was unworkable, impossible to compute, and would confuse consumers.

Open Food Facts immediately contacted the EREN research team, obtained the mathematical algorithm, and computed the 5-color score across all products in our open database overnight. We proved to regulators, journalists, and consumers that the score was completely practical and immediately usable.

2. Breaking Down Barriers for Food Producers

To accelerate adoption, Open Food Facts built the free Open Food Facts Pro platform. Manufacturers can upload recipes, run instant Nutri-Score simulations, test reformulation scenarios (e.g. reducing salt by 10% to move from C to B), and publish verified data directly to citizens.

3. Documenting Industrial Reformulation

Because Open Food Facts preserves historical product revisions, our database enabled researchers to track the Nutri-Score reformulation wave. Supermarkets (Intermarché, Delhaize, Albert Heijn) and major manufacturers reformulated thousands of product recipes to eliminate saturated fats, sugar, and excess salt so they could proudly display an A or B on packaging.

4. The Foundation for European Health Tech

Every mobile application that scans food barcodes today—including popular shopping aids, healthcare apps, and university diet tools—relies directly or indirectly on the Open Food Facts Nutri-Score calculation engine.

The 360° Food Evaluation Matrix

Understanding how each score answers a different critical dimension of food.

A food product cannot be judged on a single metric alone. A product can be nutritionally balanced yet ultra-processed; another may be organic yet packaged in non-recyclable plastic. Open Food Facts brings all dimensions together:

Indicator What It Evaluates Scale & Output Key Scientific Inputs Why It Matters
🥗 Nutri-Score Overall nutritional balance and nutrient density 5 Letters: A to E (Color coded) Calories, saturated fat, sugars, sodium, fiber, protein, fruits/vegetables/nuts percentage Prevents chronic diet-related diseases (obesity, cardiovascular disease, hypertension)
🏭 NOVA / UPF Degree and purpose of industrial processing 4 Groups: 1 to 4 (Numeric) Ingredient processing markers, industrial additives, texturizers, emulsifiers Limits metabolic disruption, cancer correlation, and hyper-palatable industrial foods
🌱 Green-Score Comprehensive ecological and environmental footprint 5 Letters: A to E (Leaf badge) ADEME Agribalyse LCA (16 PEF indicators), labels, transport freight, packaging circularity, EPI Combats climate change, water depletion, chemical pollution, and packaging waste
🌳 Forest Footprint Embedded deforestation and habitat destruction Deforestation risk score / impact Envol Vert data, poultry/egg feed soy conversion, origin geolocation, deforestation-free labels Protects tropical rainforests (Amazon, Cerrado) and vulnerable indigenous habitats
🚦 UK Traffic Lights Specific macronutrient warning cut-offs Color Pills: Green, Amber, Red UK FSA thresholds per 100g and portion for fat, saturated fat, sugars, and salt Gives instantaneous red-flag warnings for specific health conditions (diabetes, salt restrictions)
The 360° Perspective in Practice:
Take an organic chocolate cookie: It may score Nutri-Score D (high in sugar and butter), NOVA 3 (artisanal processed food without cosmetic additives), and Green-Score B (organic ingredients with recyclable paper box). None of these ratings contradict each other—they reveal the complete truth of the food you eat.
